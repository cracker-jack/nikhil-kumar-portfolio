Access is not the same as adoption.

Free trials lower the barrier to trying a product. But enabling a trial is only one step: tenants still need to discover the offering, begin using it, and find a reason to return.

The program spanned 16 offerings: 8 AI and 8 security and governance products, targeting 5,000 enterprise and SMB tenants. The engineering challenge included making the launch measurable beyond discovery alone.

My contribution

I launched free trials across the 16 offerings and built 9+ weeks of conversion telemetry to follow the path from trial access to consumption.

I flagged tenants using 80% or more of their free units for sales follow-up and surfaced engagement drop-offs in weeks 2–5, informing retention efforts.

The platform behind the experience

The platform supports tenant-scoped offer lifecycle APIs and consumption tracking. Those capabilities provide the context for the trial experience and adoption measurements.

Scope: my contribution is the trial launch and telemetry described here. Tenant-scoped offer APIs and consumption tracking are platform capabilities, not a claim that I personally authored every underlying component.

Read the funnel with its context.

In-product trial discovery reached 3,981 of the 5,000 targeted tenants. Active consuming tenants grew from 77 to 234 in six weeks, approximately 3× growth and 4.7% of the targeted tenant base.

The strongest individual product funnel achieved 87.9% trial-to-active-use. That is a product-level result, not a conversion rate for the whole program.

These signals describe discovery and product consumption. They do not establish paid conversion or a revenue outcome.

Turn measurement into a next action.

  1. Look past the initial trial start

    The 9+ week telemetry window made it possible to see adoption over time rather than relying on launch-day activity.

  2. Surface meaningful consumption

    Flagging tenants at 80%+ free-unit usage gave sales a concrete usage signal for follow-up, without assuming that usage guaranteed a purchase.

  3. Find the engagement gaps

    Drop-offs during weeks 2–5 identified a period to focus retention efforts. Multi-product adoption showed how consuming tenants explored the broader offering set.

The most useful adoption metric is one that tells you what to investigate or do next.
